## Introduction

Selecting the correct industrial lubricant is one of the most cost-effective decisions a maintenance team can make. An estimated 40% of bearing failures and 70% of hydraulic system failures are directly linked to improper lubrication — wrong viscosity, contaminated fluid, or incompatible additive chemistry.

At BizHero Lubricants, we carry a full portfolio of **Valvoline**, **PETRONAS**, and **Monroe** products so you always have the right fluid for the job. This guide walks through the key factors to consider when choosing industrial lubricants for your facility.

## 1. Understand Your Equipment Specifications

Always start with the OEM manual. The manufacturer defines:

- **Viscosity grade** (ISO VG for industrial oils, NLGI grade for greases)
- **Base oil type** (mineral, semi-synthetic, or full synthetic)
- **Required performance standards** (DIN 51524, ISO 6743, AGMA, etc.)
- **Operating temperature range**

Using a lubricant outside these specs voids warranties and accelerates wear. If you are unsure, our technical team can cross-reference OEM requirements with available PETRONAS and Valvoline grades.

## 2. Match Viscosity to Operating Conditions

Viscosity is the single most important property. Oil that is too thin fails to maintain a protective film; oil that is too thick increases energy consumption and generates excess heat.

| Application | Typical ISO VG Range |
|---|---|
| Hydraulic systems (low pressure) | ISO VG 32 – 46 |
| Hydraulic systems (high pressure) | ISO VG 46 – 68 |
| Industrial gearboxes | ISO VG 150 – 460 |
| Air compressors | ISO VG 46 – 100 |
| Slideways | ISO VG 68 – 220 |

For equipment exposed to wide temperature swings, consider multi-grade oils or high-VI (Viscosity Index) formulations. **PETRONAS Hydraulic Oil HLP** and **Valvoline HLP-D** both deliver excellent shear stability across a broad temperature range.

## 3. Evaluate Additive Requirements

Base oil alone cannot protect against wear, rust, oxidation, and foaming. Additive packages are tailored to specific applications:

- **AW (Anti-Wear)** — Zinc-based compounds for hydraulic pumps and vane motors
- **EP (Extreme Pressure)** — Sulfur-phosphorus additives for heavily loaded gears
- **R&O (Rust and Oxidation)** — For circulating systems, turbines, and compressors
- **Detergent / Dispersant** — Keeps contaminants in suspension in engine oils

Choosing the wrong additive package can cause seal incompatibility, deposit formation, or even corrosive attack on yellow metals. Always verify additive compatibility with your seals and metallurgy.

## 4. Select the Right Grease

Grease selection involves three choices:

1. **Thickener type** — Lithium, lithium-complex, calcium-sulfonate, polyurea, or aluminium-complex
2. **Base oil viscosity** — Typically ISO VG 100 to 460
3. **NLGI consistency grade** — NLGI #2 is the most common general-purpose grade; NLGI #1 for centralised systems, NLGI #3 for high-temperature or vertical applications

**Valvoline Multipurpose Grease EP** and the **PETRONAS Grease GEP** range cover most industrial bearing and chassis applications.

## 5. Implement a Condition Monitoring Program

Even the best lubricant degrades over time. Establish a routine oil analysis schedule to monitor:

- Viscosity change (oxidation, shearing, or contamination)
- Acid number (TAN) — indicates oxidation
- Water content — promotes rust and additive depletion
- Wear metals — early warning of mechanical failure

Our **Used Oil Analysis** service helps you trend these parameters so you can extend drain intervals safely and catch problems before they become failures.
