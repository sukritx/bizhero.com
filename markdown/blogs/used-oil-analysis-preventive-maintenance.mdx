## Introduction

Used oil analysis (UOA) is one of the most powerful yet underutilised tools in industrial maintenance. Often described as a blood test for machinery, UOA provides a detailed look inside your equipment without ever opening a cover plate or inspection hatch.

## What Used Oil Analysis Measures

A standard UOA report covers three primary areas:

### 1. Wear Metals (PPM)

Spectrometric analysis detects microscopic metal particles suspended in the oil, indicating which components are wearing and at what rate.

| Metal | Common Source |
|---|---|
| Iron (Fe) | Gears, cylinders, crankshafts |
| Copper (Cu) | Bushings, thrust washers, coolers |
| Aluminium (Al) | Pistons, blowers, bearing cages |
| Chromium (Cr) | Rings, liners, roller bearings |
| Lead (Pb) | Babbitt bearings, coatings |
| Silicon (Si) | Abrasive dirt ingress, seal material |

A sudden spike in any metal is a leading indicator of an impending failure. Trending these values over multiple samples is far more valuable than a single snapshot.

### 2. Fluid Condition

- **Viscosity** — Increases indicate oxidation or heavy soot loading; decreases indicate fuel dilution or shearing
- **Total Acid Number (TAN)** — Rising TAN signals oxidation and additive depletion in industrial oils
- **Total Base Number (TBN)** — Declining TBN in engine oils indicates reduced ability to neutralise acidic combustion by-products
- **FTIR Oxidation / Nitration** — Direct measurement of oil degradation chemistry

### 3. Contamination

- **Water** — Promotes rust, additive dropout, and hydrogen embrittlement; measured in ppm or by Karl Fischer titration
- **Fuel Dilution** — Reduces viscosity and flash point in engine oils
- **Glycol** — Indicates coolant leaks; catastrophic for bearings
- **Soot** — Abrasive carbon particles from incomplete combustion
- **Particle Count (ISO 4406)** — Measures cleanliness; critical for hydraulic and turbine systems

## How Often Should You Sample?

| Equipment Type | Recommended Frequency |
|---|---|
| Large diesel / gas engines | Every 250 – 500 hours |
| Hydraulic systems | Quarterly |
| Gearboxes (critical) | Quarterly |
| Gearboxes (non-critical) | Semi-annually |
| Compressors | Quarterly |
| Turbines | Monthly to quarterly |

High-value or safety-critical assets should be sampled more frequently. BizHero can help you establish a sampling schedule based on your specific equipment, operating environment, and criticality ranking.

## Real Savings from Oil Analysis

Facilities that adopt a structured UOA program typically see:

- **20–50% reduction in unscheduled downtime** — Problems are caught during planned windows, not on the night shift
- **Extended drain intervals** — Safely stretch oil changes by 2–3× when analysis confirms the oil is still serviceable
- **Lower lubricant consumption** — Top up by condition, not by calendar
- **Fewer catastrophic failures** — Replace a $50 bearing before it destroys a $5,000 shaft
- **Reduced spare parts inventory** — Move from reactive to predictive maintenance
